# Insights

_Generated by `python -m aqip insights` from the warehouse; numbers are recomputed on every run._

## 1. Delhi is the most polluted city by a wide margin
Delhi averages PM2.5 of 83.5 µg/m³ (AQI 239); Bengaluru averages 22.1, a 3.8x gap. Delhi exceeds the WHO 24-hour guideline (15 µg/m³) on 100% of days and is Poor or worse on 53% of days. Even Bengaluru exceeds the WHO guideline on 60% of days.

## 2. Seasonality: winter is the worst season in 10 of 10 cities
The largest winter-to-monsoon PM2.5 ratios are Pune 4.5x, Kolkata 4.0x, Bengaluru 3.3x. In Delhi: winter 106, post-monsoon 73, summer 81, monsoon 66 µg/m³. Delhi stays heavily polluted all year, so its seasonal ratio is modest even though its absolute level is highest.

## 3. Still air traps pollution; wind and rain clear it
Across all cities, hours with calm wind (<5 km/h) average PM2.5 of 67.3 µg/m³ versus 22.6 when breezy (12+ km/h). Daily wind speed correlates with PM2.5 at r = -0.2 in Delhi and -0.46 in Mumbai. Outside the monsoon, days with ≥2 mm rain are 43% lower in PM2.5 than dry days (median across cities).

## 4. Diwali: no consistent spike in this dataset
Delhi PM2.5 during the Diwali window (day ±1) versus the surrounding weeks: 2025: +25% (90.0 vs 72.2 µg/m³), 2024: -39% (66.2 vs 107.9 µg/m³). Median across all cities by year: 2024: -9%, 2025: +20%. Caveat: Open-Meteo air quality comes from the CAMS atmospheric model, which does not resolve firework emissions; station-level CPCB/OpenAQ data would show the sharper spike. The pipeline is source-agnostic, so swapping the ingest step is the upgrade path.

## 5. Weekdays vs weekends, and the daily rhythm
Weekend PM2.5 differs from weekdays by -1.0% (median across cities), which is small next to the seasonal swings. Delhi's hourly pattern peaks at 23:00 (102.0 µg/m³) and is lowest at 15:00 (58.5 µg/m³) (times are IST).
